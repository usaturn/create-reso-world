# Modular Environment Design

Use a project-defined grid and repeatable connection logic so pieces can combine without forcing every asset to the same dimensions. Choose a module unit that fits the intended building and circulation; document its origin, snap axes, floor datum and any sub-grid. Keep origins and interfaces consistent for interchangeable pieces.

## Kit roles

- **Corner:** turns or terminates two runs; provide mirrored or handed variants when orientation matters.
- **Edge:** straight wall, floor or ceiling run, with lengths that compose cleanly on the chosen grid.
- **Fill:** uninterrupted panel or infill between structure, openings or other modules.
- **Trim:** shared transition, cap, sill, base, cornice or joint detail. Decide whether it is geometry or a reusable surface region based on depth and visibility.
- **Opening:** door, window, arch or service access. Define the clear opening, frame depth, sill/lintel interface and adjacent wall thickness together.

## Compatibility checks

1. Keep corner and edge profiles aligned: thickness, height, datum and surface offsets must meet without gaps or overlaps.
2. Make fill pieces occupy the same bounded span as their surrounding frame; define how optional trim covers or reveals the joint.
3. Size openings from their usable clear dimensions, not only the decorative frame. Check that wall, floor and ceiling modules terminate cleanly around them.
4. Verify both inside and outside corners, handed rotations, T-junctions and end caps. A kit should not require hidden one-off patches at every turn.
5. Establish tolerances appropriate to the project scale and placement method. Inspect assembled seams in a test arrangement; do not treat one numeric tolerance as universal.

This reference covers module taxonomy and interface compatibility only. It does not prescribe world blockout, composition or runtime layout.
